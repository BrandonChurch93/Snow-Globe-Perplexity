Image 1 is a rough composite of exactly the photograph that is wanted: a snow globe an instant after it was shaken hard and set back down on a black polished surface. The globe itself stands perfectly still and upright, but the liquid inside it is still spinning fast round the vertical axis of the globe, and dandelion seeds and fireflies fill the whole globe as a storm of motion-blurred streaks, so thick that the miniature inside is half lost behind it. In image 1 the storm was painted in by hand. Photograph the same moment for real, at the same strength: every painted streak becomes a real dandelion seed, or the light trail of a real firefly smeared by motion blur, the painted haze becomes real seed fluff glowing gold in the low sun, and nothing looks painted any more.

AS THICK AS IMAGE 1. This is the one thing that must not change. Wherever the storm covers the miniature in image 1, it covers it in the result, just as thickly. Do not clear the storm away from the cottage or the tree, do not thin it, do not calm it, and do not reveal anything that image 1 hides. The first thing the eye sees is a globe full of whirling seeds and sparks of light; only then does it find the miniature inside, dim and veiled, like a house seen through a blizzard at night.

THE STORM IN THE LIQUID. Keep the number of streaks, their directions, their lengths and their three sizes as they are in image 1. Every particle is drawn out along its path by its speed; none hangs still and sharp. All the streaks follow one coherent rotation round the vertical axis of the globe: they run in long shallow arcs that bend with the curve of the glass, nearly level across the middle of the globe and curving round at its left and right edges, so the whole body of liquid is seen to turn, a vortex viewed from the side. There are three depths. Close against the front glass: a few large, long, soft, out-of-focus streaks. At the distance of the cottage and the tree: a great many fine streaks with crisp edges, each one a short curved dash, crossing in front of the cottage, the tree and the lamppost. Behind the miniature: the far side of the swirl, a turning haze of very fine faint streaks. Thicker bands of storm wind round the globe like the arms of a spiral, with thinner gaps between them. The storm fills the whole globe, from the front glass to the back glass and from the ground to the very top of the dome, right out to the glass on every side, with no calm, dark, empty patch anywhere in the liquid.

THE PARTICLES. Dandelion seeds and fireflies: a golden storm. Many thousands of dandelion seeds, white feathery parachutes backlit by the low sun, every one smeared into a fine pale-gold streak with a faint feathered edge, so thick that between and behind them the liquid is a whirling golden-white haze. Among them the fireflies are drawn out into short curved trails of yellow-green light, bright thin glowing lines with a soft halo, each brightest at its head, tracing the rotation like sparks in a long exposure; they are scattered through the whole globe at every depth, the near ones soft and wide, the far ones hair-fine. A few small green leaves torn from the tree spin with them.

WHAT STILL SHOWS OF THE MINIATURE. Only this, and only dimly, through the gaps between the streaks: the slate roof and the gable of the cottage, the string of paper lanterns burning as soft orange and amber halos between the cottage and the tree, the lit windows, the lamppost, and the deep green crown of the tree as a broken dark shape behind the gold. The fence, the path, the sunflowers, the pond and the foot of the tree are lost in the whirling seeds. What does show stands exactly where it stands in image 1 and is not motion-blurred itself, but it is pale and low in contrast behind the storm. Light from inside the globe carries through the storm: the lanterns, the windows and the lamppost glow, and the seeds that pass close to them light up, so the heart of the golden storm glows like a lit garden party seen through blowing fluff.

MOTION BLUR ON THE PARTICLES ONLY. Only what the liquid carries is streaked. The miniature, the glass, the sun flare, the base, the surface and the background have no motion blur at all. The camera has not moved and the globe has not moved.

EVERYTHING OUTSIDE THE LIQUID STAYS EXACTLY AS IT IS IN IMAGE 1: the glass sphere with its thickness, its rim and its reflections and the hot star of the low sun flaring on its upper right edge; the base in every detail, the warm carved sandstone, the seashells and the frosted sea glass set into it and the sand drifting off its edge; the black polished surface with its reflection and the scatter of sand and the warm caustics lying on it; the dark background and its soft bokeh; the colour and direction of the light; the depth of field and the film grain. Nowhere on the glass is there a reflection of a window or of a room. Nothing new stands outside the glass, and the air outside the glass is completely empty: every particle is inside the liquid.
