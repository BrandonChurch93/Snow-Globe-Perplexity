Image 1 is a photograph of a snow globe an instant after it was shaken hard and set back down on a black polished surface. The globe itself stands perfectly still and upright, but the liquid inside it is still spinning fast round the vertical axis of the globe and carries the dandelion seeds and the fireflies round with it. The storm inside the liquid has been roughly painted in by hand as a guide: it shows where the streaks run, how long they are, how thick the storm is and how much of the miniature it hides. Photograph the same moment for real, so that every painted streak becomes a real dandelion seed, or the light trail of a real firefly, caught by motion blur, and nothing looks painted any more.

Same wide 16:9 framing as image 1, with the globe at exactly the same size and in exactly the same position.

THE STORM IN THE LIQUID. Keep the number of streaks, their directions, their lengths and their three sizes as they are in image 1. Do not thin the storm out, do not calm it, and do not turn the streaks back into sharp still particles: every particle is drawn out along its path by its speed. All the streaks follow one coherent rotation round the vertical axis of the globe. They run in long shallow arcs that bend with the curve of the glass, nearly level across the middle of the globe and curving round at its left and right edges, so the whole body of liquid is seen to turn, like a slow tornado viewed from the side. There are three depths. Close against the front glass: a few large, long, soft, out-of-focus streaks. At the distance of the cottage and the tree: a great many fine streaks with crisp edges, each one a short curved dash. Behind the miniature: the far side of the swirl, a turning haze of very fine faint streaks. The storm fills the whole globe, from the front glass to the back glass and from the ground to the very top of the dome, right out to the glass on every side, with no calm, dark, empty patch anywhere in the liquid.

THE PARTICLES. Dandelion seeds and fireflies: a golden storm. Many thousands of dandelion seeds, white feathery parachutes backlit by the low sun, every one smeared into a fine pale-gold streak with a faint feathered edge, so thick that the far ones turn the liquid into a whirling golden-white haze. Among them the fireflies are drawn out into short curved trails of yellow-green light, bright thin glowing lines with a soft halo, each brightest at its head, tracing the rotation like sparks in a long exposure; they are scattered through the whole globe at every depth, the near ones soft and wide, the far ones hair-fine. A few small green leaves torn from the tree spin with them.

THE SCENE IS HALF HIDDEN. The cottage, the tree, the lamppost, the fence, the path and the pond stand in exactly the same places and at the same sizes as in image 2, and they are not blurred themselves: where they show between the streaks they are sharp. But the storm covers about half of them, as in image 1. The fence, the path, the sunflowers, the pond and the foot of the tree are lost in the whirling seeds; the slate roof, the chimney and the deep green crown of the tree show through gaps in the streaks, veiled by gold. Light from inside the globe carries through the storm: the string of paper lanterns burns as soft orange and amber halos, the lit windows and the lamppost glow, and the seeds that pass close to them light up, so the heart of the golden storm glows like a lit garden party seen through blowing fluff.

MOTION BLUR ON THE PARTICLES ONLY. Only what the liquid carries is streaked. The miniature, the glass, the sun flare, the base, the surface and the background have no motion blur at all. The camera has not moved and the globe has not moved.
