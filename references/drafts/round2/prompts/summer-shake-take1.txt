THE PARTICLES. Dandelion seeds and fireflies: a golden storm. At least ten times as many dandelion seeds as in image 1, many thousands of white feathery parachutes, backlit gold by the low sun and streaked into fine curved pale-gold dashes, so thick that they turn the liquid into a whirling golden-white haze. Among them the fireflies, two hundred or more, are pulled into long curved trails of yellow-green light, bright glowing lines that trace the rotation like sparks in a long exposure. A few green leaves torn from the tree spin with them.

THE VORTEX. The whole body of liquid turns round the vertical axis of the globe, and the particles show it: they are drawn as motion-blurred streaks, each one a short curved dash smeared along its path. All the streaks follow one coherent rotation. In the front half of the globe they sweep from left to right in long shallow arcs that bend with the curve of the glass; behind the miniature they run back the other way, fainter. The swirl is a rising spiral: it lifts off the ground of the miniature at the lower left, winds up and round the cottage and the tree, tightens toward the top of the dome and curls over there, like a small tornado seen from the side. Streaks close against the front glass are large, long, soft and out of focus; streaks at the distance of the cottage and the tree are fine and crisp-edged; behind them the far side of the swirl is a pale, milky, turning haze. The storm fills the globe completely, from the front glass to the back glass and from the ground to the top of the dome, with no calm empty patch anywhere in the liquid.

THE SCENE IS HALF HIDDEN. The cottage, the tree, the lamppost, the fence, the path and the pond stand in exactly the same places and at the same sizes as in image 1, and they are not blurred themselves: where they show, they are sharp. But the storm covers about half of them. The lower walls of the cottage, the fence, the path, the sunflowers, the pond and the foot of the tree are lost in the whirling seeds; the roof, the chimney and the dark green crown of the tree show through gaps in the streaks, veiled by gold. Light from inside the globe carries through the storm: the paper lanterns burn as soft red, orange and amber halos, the lit windows and the lamppost glow, and the seeds that pass close to them light up, so the heart of the golden storm glows like a lit festival seen through blowing fluff.
