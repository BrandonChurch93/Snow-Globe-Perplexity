Image 1 is a finished photograph of a snow globe in high summer at golden dusk, standing on a black polished surface. Keep everything in it exactly as it is: the same wide 16:9 framing, the globe at exactly the same size and in exactly the same position, the same heavy clear glass sphere with its thickness and rim and the hot sun flare with its rays on the upper right edge, the same miniature inside object for object (stone cottage with slate roof, chimney and climbing roses on the left, big deep green tree on the right, black iron lamppost, wooden fence, sunflowers, stepping-stone path, still mirror pond in front), the same honey sandstone base with its carved ripple layers, the same black polished surface with its scatter of pale sand and its golden reflections, the same out-of-focus background and golden bokeh, the same low golden dusk light, the same shallow depth of field and film grain.

Make only these five changes.

1. FAR MORE PARTICLES IN THE LIQUID. Dandelion seeds: about fifteen times as many as in image 1, at least a hundred and fifty, each a fine stalk under a feathery white parachute of filaments, backlit so the filaments glow gold, drifting slowly at every depth from the front glass to the back glass and from the grass to the very top of the dome, with no empty patches. Fireflies: about three times as many as in image 1, tiny points of warm yellow-green light with soft halos. The depth is unmistakable, in three layers. Front layer, close against the front glass: twenty or more large, soft, out-of-focus dandelion seeds, three or four times bigger than the crisp ones, and a dozen fireflies as big soft glowing yellow-green discs, spread over the whole face of the globe. Middle layer, at the distance of the cottage and the tree: many small crisp seeds and bright small fireflies, some crossing in front of the cottage roof, the tree's crown and trunk and the lamppost. Far layer, behind the tree and the chimney: a fine haze of tiny faint seeds and pinprick fireflies. None is streaked or motion-blurred. Every seed and every firefly is inside the glass; the air outside the glass stays completely empty.

2. PAPER LANTERNS. The small round lights become real paper lanterns, larger and fewer: round ribbed paper globes with visible bamboo ribs and a small cap, in red, orange, amber and cream, each about as wide as the cottage door, each lit from inside by a small flame and glowing. Seven hang on the cord that swags from the cottage eave across to the lowest branch of the tree, and six more hang on short cords among the tree's branches at different heights. The row of tiny lights along the cottage eaves is gone; the eaves are plain. The lanterns are the brightest things inside the globe; their red, orange and amber glow lights the leaves, the cottage wall and the grass near them and reflects in the pond.

3. THE REFLECTION ON THE GLASS. At the upper right of the dome there is a bright reflection crossed by dark bars, like the reflection of a window with glazing bars. Remove it completely. The sun flare and its rays stay exactly where they are; beside them there is only one long, narrow, soft curved highlight following the curve of the dome, like the highlight on the upper right of the glass in image 2. Nowhere on the glass is there a reflection of a window, a window frame or a room.

4. THE BASE, MORE SCULPTURAL. Same size, same shape, same sandstone. The smooth glossy glass beads set in it become real sea glass: frosted, matt, irregular shards and pebbles tumbled by the sea, with soft worn edges, in aqua, bottle green, amber and pale blue, translucent and softly glowing where the low sun passes through them, none shiny, none perfectly round. Many more seashells are set into the stone all the way round, bedded like a jeweller's inlay: fan scallops, ridged cockles, a spiral whelk, a cone shell, tiny periwinkles and limpets, of different sizes, with small barnacle crusts between them. The sandstone is carved by wind into deep flowing ripples and small hollows that hold sand. Dry sand is pouring off the ledge at the front right: a thin, bright, sunlit veil of falling grains drops from the rim to the polished surface and feeds a small cone of sand there. No wood of any kind.

5. CAUSTICS. The liquid-filled sphere focuses the low sun onto the black polished surface in front of and to the left of the base: a bright net of sharp, curved, golden caustic lines and one hot spot of light, lying across the gloss and the scattered sand.
