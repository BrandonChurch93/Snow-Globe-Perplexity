BASE. A sculpture of living moss, roots and stone. A drum of rough dark fieldstone, wet with rain, is almost swallowed by thick cushions of real emerald moss. Pale gnarled tree roots twist out of the moss, grip the stone like fingers and wind round the base down to the polished surface. Clusters of tiny mushrooms, ivory and tan caps on hair-thin stems, grow from the moss and the roots. One thin trickle of clear water wells from under the rim at the front left, runs down a groove in the bare wet stone as a bright glinting thread, drips off a mossy lip and gathers in a small shining puddle on the polished surface at the foot. Beads of dew sit on the moss. The glass sits in a rim of mossy stone. No flowers on the base, no lettering.

GLASS. Dew and rain on the outside of the glass. Fine dew beads the dome, heaviest toward the top and the sides. Among it sit larger rain drops, and many of them have run: thin clear vertical trails cut down through the dew, each trail ending in a fat hanging drop, a few trails joining. The middle of the glass stays clear enough to see the whole scene. No frost.

INSIDE. Early spring, just after dawn. The big tree is in full pink blossom, a dense cloud of cherry blossom. Tulips in red, pink and yellow stand along the path, the fence and the edge of the pond. Fresh bright grass with tiny daisies; moss on the cottage roof. The pond has thawed: clear dark water with fine concentric ripples. A soft dawn mist lies low over the grass and the pond and drifts between the cottage and the tree. Light comes from inside the globe: the cottage's small leaded windows glow warm amber, and the lamppost is lit, a warm glowing lantern with a soft halo in the mist, its light lying on the path and reflected in the pond. Plain oak door, no wreath. No smoke.

PARTICLES. Blossom petals, far denser than in image 2: many hundreds of small pale pink petals suspended in the liquid at every depth, drifting and tumbling slowly, from the front glass to the back glass and from the grass to the top of the dome, with no empty patches. The depth is unmistakable, in three layers. Front layer, close against the front glass: fifty or more large, soft, out-of-focus petals, pale pink blurred shapes several times bigger than the crisp ones, spread over the whole face of the globe. Middle layer, at the distance of the cottage and the tree: hundreds of small crisp petals, each a real curled petal with a paler base, some crossing in front of the cottage and the tree. Far layer, behind the tree: a fine scatter of tiny faint petals. Petals near the lamppost and the windows catch warm light. The cottage, tree, pond, lamppost, fence and path stay clearly readable through the petals. Every drifting petal is inside the glass; nothing floats in the air outside it.

ON THE SURFACE. Fallen petals. Pale pink blossom petals lie scattered on the black polished surface around the foot of the base, thickest near the base and thinning further out, each with its soft reflection in the gloss, with a few beads of water among them. Nothing else lies on the surface.

LIGHT AND BACKGROUND. Soft pink morning light. A gentle rose-pink key from high on the right draws a long soft pink highlight down the upper right of the dome and a thin pale rim along its left edge, and lays a faint rose sheen on the polished surface. The background is soft out-of-focus darkness in deep plum and dusky rose, with large soft round bokeh discs of pale pink, peach and warm cream light and a few soft spring green ones, sparse and of different sizes. The warm amber light from the cottage windows and the lamppost glows through the glass and spills a soft warm pool onto the moss and the surface in front. Gentle contrast.
