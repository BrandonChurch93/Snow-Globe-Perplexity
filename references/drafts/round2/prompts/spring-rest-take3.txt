THE PETALS IN THE LIQUID. Keep their number, their positions and their three sizes as in image 1: a thick, slow flurry of about six hundred blossom petals that fills the liquid from the front glass to the back glass and from the grass to the very top of the dome, as dense as a heavy snowfall, with no empty patches. Do not thin them out and do not remove any. Render every one as a real cherry blossom petal hanging in clear liquid: thin, curled, slightly translucent, pale pink with a paler base and a small notch at the tip, each turned at its own angle, lit by the pink morning light and, near the cottage and the lamppost, by warm gold. The largest petals, close against the front glass, are big soft out-of-focus pale pink shapes. The mid-sized ones, at the distance of the cottage and the tree, are crisp. The smallest, behind the tree and the chimney, are a fine faint haze. Petals cross in front of the roof, the tree's crown and trunk, the lamppost and the pond, so the miniature is seen through the flurry and stays clearly readable. No petal is streaked or motion-blurred. Every drifting petal is inside the glass; the air outside the glass is completely empty.

RAIN ON THE GLASS. On the outside of the glass, among the fine dew, rain beads have run down the left third and the right third of the dome: thin clear trails wiped through the dew, following the curve of the glass downward, each ending in a fat hanging drop that refracts the scene and carries one bright point of light; a few trails join. Real water on real glass, never drawn lines. The middle of the dome stays clear enough to see the whole scene.

Nowhere on the glass is there a reflection of a window, a window frame or a room; only soft curved highlights.
