THE STORM IN THE LIQUID. Keep the number of streaks, their directions, their lengths and their three sizes as they are in image 1. Do not thin the storm out, do not calm it, and do not turn the streaks back into sharp still particles: every particle is drawn out along its path by its speed. All the streaks follow one coherent rotation round the vertical axis of the globe. They run in long shallow arcs that bend with the curve of the glass, nearly level across the middle of the globe and curving round at its left and right edges, so the whole body of liquid is seen to turn, like a slow tornado viewed from the side. There are three depths. Close against the front glass: a few large, long, soft, out-of-focus streaks. At the distance of the cottage and the tree: a great many fine streaks with crisp edges, each one a short curved dash. Behind the miniature: the far side of the swirl, a turning haze of very fine faint streaks. The storm fills the whole globe, from the front glass to the back glass and from the ground to the very top of the dome, right out to the glass on every side, with no calm, dark, empty patch anywhere in the liquid.

THE PARTICLES. Snow: a blizzard, a near white-out. Many thousands of flakes, from fine dust to fat clumps, every one a white streak. The fine far haze is real powder snow turning in the liquid, milky and soft. The shaking has torn the loose snow off the ground of the miniature: a thick cloud of white powder boils up from the bottom of the globe and is drawn up into the rotation, so the lowest part of the liquid is almost solid white. The flakes are cold white and blue-white where the evening light catches them and turn warm gold where they pass the lights of the miniature.

THE SCENE IS HALF HIDDEN. The cottage, the tree, the lamppost, the fence, the path and the pond stand in exactly the same places and at the same sizes as in image 2, and they are not blurred themselves: where they show between the streaks they are sharp. But the storm covers about half of them, as in image 1. The fence, the path, the pond, the foot of the tree and the lower walls of the cottage are lost in the churned-up snow; the roof, the gable, the chimney, the crown of the tree and the star show through gaps in the streaks, veiled by white. Light from inside the globe carries through the storm: the string lights on the eaves and in the tree, the star, the windows and the lamppost burn as soft warm amber halos, and the flakes that pass close to them light up gold, so the heart of the white storm glows warm against the cold blue of the glass.

MOTION BLUR ON THE PARTICLES ONLY. Only what the liquid carries is streaked. The miniature, the glass, the frost on the glass, the base, the surface and the background have no motion blur at all. The camera has not moved and the globe has not moved.
