1. LIGHT FROM INSIDE THE GLOBE. It is just after dawn and the cottage is lit from within. Both small windows glow warm amber-gold, clearly much brighter than the stone around them, as if a lamp and a fire burn inside; their light falls on the flowers and the grass beneath them. The lamppost is lit: the lantern at its top burns with a bright warm golden flame, with a soft round halo around it, a pool of warm light on the path and the grass at its foot and a golden reflection trembling in the pond. The windows and the lantern are the warmest, brightest points in the frame, and a soft warm glow from them reaches the glass.

2. DAWN MIST. A soft, low, pale mist lies over the grass and the pond and drifts between the cottage and the tree, thickest near the ground and behind the cottage, glowing warm where the lamppost and the windows light it. The cottage, the tree and the pond stay clearly readable.

3. FAR MORE PETALS IN THE LIQUID. About six times as many blossom petals as in image 1: a thick, slow flurry, as dense as a heavy snowfall, at least five hundred petals, filling the liquid from the front glass to the back glass and from the grass to the very top of the dome, with no empty patches. The depth is unmistakable, in three layers. Front layer, close against the front glass: sixty or more large, soft, out-of-focus petals, pale pink blurred shapes three or four times bigger than the crisp ones, spread over the whole face of the globe. Middle layer, at the distance of the cottage and the tree: hundreds of small crisp petals, each a real curled petal with a paler base, many crossing in front of the cottage roof, the tree trunk and crown, the lamppost and the pond. Far layer, behind the tree and the chimney: a fine haze of tiny faint petals. Petals near the lamppost and the windows catch warm gold light. They drift and tumble slowly; none is streaked or motion-blurred. Every drifting petal is inside the glass; the air outside the glass stays completely empty.

4. THE REFLECTION ON THE GLASS. At the upper right of the dome there is a bright reflection crossed by dark bars, like the reflection of a window with glazing bars. Remove it completely. In its place there is only one long, narrow, soft curved highlight that follows the curve of the dome, like the highlight on the upper right of the glass in image 2. Nowhere on the glass is there a reflection of a window, a window frame or a room.

5. RAIN ON THE GLASS. Among the fine dew on the outside of the glass, a dozen or more rain beads have run down: thin clear vertical trails cut through the dew, each ending in a fat hanging drop, a few trails joining. They sit mostly on the left third and the right third of the dome; the middle stays clear enough to see the whole scene.
