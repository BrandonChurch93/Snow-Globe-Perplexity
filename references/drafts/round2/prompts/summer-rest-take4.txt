Make one change, on the base only: THE BAND OF SHELLS AND SEA GLASS SET INTO THE SANDSTONE.

1. Sea glass. On the band there are two flat pale turquoise triangles that look like cut paper, and several glossy clear droplets and round beads. Remove all of them. In their place, and between the shells, sit ten to twelve pieces of real beach sea glass: frosted, matt and cloudy like sugared glass, never glossy, never clear like a water drop and never perfectly round; irregular tumbled chunks and shards with softened edges and real thickness, each half sunk into the stone with a little sand banked against it, in pale aqua, seafoam green, deep bottle green, one cobalt blue and one honey amber. The low sun passes through their edges so they glow softly from inside, and each throws a small shadow.

2. Seashells. About twice as many as now, eighteen or more, of different kinds and sizes, each set flush into the stone: ribbed scallops and cockles, small spiral whelks, top shells and a few tiny cowries, in ivory, sand-pink and pale apricot, with fine ribs and growth lines. The shells already there stay where they are.

3. The stone. The wind-carved layers of the sandstone are cut deeper and more sculptural: undercut ledges, small hollows, ripple marks and a few pits, with loose sand lying in the hollows and on the ledges.

Nothing else changes anywhere in the picture. No driftwood. Nothing new on the polished surface and nothing in the air outside the glass.
