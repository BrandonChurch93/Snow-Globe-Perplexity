The globe itself stands perfectly still and upright, but the liquid inside it is still spinning fast round the vertical axis of the globe, and dry leaves, embers and green fog fill the whole globe as a storm of motion-blurred streaks, so thick that the miniature inside is half lost behind it. In image 1 the storm was painted in by hand. Photograph the same moment for real, at the same strength: every painted streak becomes a real autumn leaf, or the light trail of a real ember smeared by motion blur, the painted haze becomes real smoke-brown murk and glowing green fog, and nothing looks painted any more.

AS THICK AS IMAGE 1. This is the one thing that must not change. Wherever the storm covers the miniature in image 1, it covers it in the result, just as thickly. THE STORM IN THE LIQUID. Keep the number of streaks, their directions, their lengths and their three sizes as they are in image 1. Every particle is drawn out along its path by its speed; none hangs still and sharp. All the streaks follow one coherent rotation round the vertical axis of the globe: they run in long shallow arcs that bend with the curve of the glass, nearly level across the middle of the globe and curving round at its left and right edges, so the whole body of liquid is seen to turn, a vortex viewed from the side. There are three depths. Close against the front glass: a few large, long, soft, out-of-focus streaks. At the distance of the cottage and the tree: a great many fine streaks with crisp edges, each one a short curved dash, crossing in front of the cottage, the tree and the lamppost. Behind the miniature: the far side of the swirl, a turning haze of very fine faint streaks. Thicker bands of storm wind round the globe like the arms of a spiral, with thinner gaps between them. The storm fills the whole globe, from the front glass to the back glass and from the ground to the very top of the dome, right out to the glass on every side, with no calm, dark, empty patch anywhere in the liquid.

THE PARTICLES. Leaves, embers and fog: a firestorm of autumn. Many thousands of dry leaves, orange, rust, red and yellow, every one smeared into a curved streak of its own colour; the large ones near the front glass are broad soft orange smears. Among them hundreds of embers are drawn out into short curved trails of orange-gold light, bright thin glowing lines with a soft halo, each brightest at its head, tracing the rotation like sparks whirled off a bonfire in a long exposure. The green fog that lay pooled at the bottom of the globe has been whipped up into the rotation: luminous toxic-green ribbons of mist wind up through the orange storm from the ground to the top of the dome and glow from within, brightest low down against the glass. Five or six tiny black bats are flung round near the top of the dome, each smeared a little by its own motion.

WHAT STILL SHOWS OF THE MINIATURE. Only this, and only dimly, through the gaps between the streaks: the crooked slate roof and the chimney of the cottage, one window glowing sickly green and one violet purple, the lamppost as an orange blur, the jack-o-lanterns as soft orange halos, and the bare crooked black branches of the tree as broken dark lines behind the leaves. The crooked fence, the path, the pond, the grave posts and the foot of the tree are lost in the turning leaves and the green fog. What does show stands exactly where it stands in image 1 and is not motion-blurred itself, but it is pale and low in contrast behind the storm. Light from inside the globe carries through the storm: the pumpkins, the windows and the lamppost glow, and the leaves that pass close to them light up, so the heart of the storm glows like a furnace seen through smoke.

MOTION BLUR ON THE PARTICLES ONLY. Only what the liquid carries is streaked. The miniature, the glass, the crack in the glass, the base, the surface and the background have no motion blur at all. The camera has not moved and the globe has not moved.
