Small jack-o-lantern faces are cut right through the iron of the drum, each with triangular eyes, a nose and a jagged grin, five or six of them round the visible side, and a fire burns inside the base, so every face glows hot orange from within and throws its shape of light onto the polished surface. Melted candle wax, ivory and yellowed, has run down over the rim and the thorns in long drips and frozen curtains, with beads hanging from the thorns and small pools hardened at the foot. A few fine cobweb strands hang between the thorns. The glass sits in a ring of hammered black iron. No pumpkin on or beside the base, no standing candles, no lettering.

GLASS. One hairline crack: a single fine fracture line, unbranched, running in one gentle curve from the upper left edge of the dome down toward the middle left, catching the light as a thin bright thread. Only one crack: no forks, no second crack, no chips, no hole. The rest of the glass is clear. Green fog pools at the bottom inside the globe: a heavy, luminous, toxic-green mist lying on the ground of the miniature, pooling over the pond, wreathing the fence and the foot of the tree, and thinning to nothing at the height of the cottage roof.

INSIDE. Halloween night. The big tree is bare, black and crooked, with clawed, twisting branches. Dead grass and fallen leaves cover the ground; the pond lies black and still under the green fog. No smoke.
