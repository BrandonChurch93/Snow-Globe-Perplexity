KEEP EXACTLY AS IN IMAGES 1 AND 2: THE MINIATURE AND THE GLASS.
The miniature inside the glass is the same diorama, object for object in the same positions, at the same relative sizes, seen from the same angle. It is a museum-grade model built from real materials at tiny scale, standing on a low mound that fills the bottom third of the sphere:
- Left of centre, set a little back: the small stone cottage in three-quarter view, walls of tiny real fieldstones with mortar joints, steep slate roof, stone chimney at the left end of the ridge, a round-topped oak plank door in the gable end facing front-right, small leaded windows glowing warm amber.
- Right of centre: the one large old tree with a thick gnarled trunk and a broad rounded crown, taller than the cottage, its top about three quarters of the way up the dome.
- In front, nearest the camera, between the cottage and the tree: the small oval pond.
- The single black iron lamppost standing just right of the cottage door, behind the pond.
- The low wooden fence along the front left of the yard.
- The short path of flat stones curving from the cottage door down to the left edge of the pond.
Winter dress as in image 1: deep snow lying thick and rounded on the roof, the fence, the branches and the ground. Warm white micro string lights along the cottage eaves and wound through the bare, snow-laden branches of the tree. A small brass star at the top of the tree. An evergreen wreath with a red ribbon on the door. A thin curl of pale smoke from the chimney. The pond frozen: grey-blue ice with frost and a dusting of snow. The lamppost lit. The path trodden through the snow.
The glass is the same hand-blown sphere filled with clear liquid, with real thickness: a bright double rim at the silhouette, lens-like refraction that slightly magnifies the scene and bends it near the edges, a tiny flattened air bubble at the very top. The same fern frost as in image 1 creeps inward over the glass from the outer edge of the dome all the way round and up from the collar: a feathery white ring of frost fronds, widest low on the left and right, leaving the middle of the glass clear.

CAMERA AND FRAMING: MUCH CLOSER THAN IMAGE 2.
The lens is slightly below the centre of the glass sphere, about level with the cottage roof, and looks horizontally, so we see into the miniature from just above its ground. The snow globe is exactly centred left to right. The whole object, from the foot of its base to the top of the glass, spans 80 percent of the frame height: one tenth of the frame height stays clear above the top of the glass, and one tenth of the frame height below the foot of the base shows the polished surface. The glass sphere is about one third of the frame width, so about one third of the frame is free on each side of it. The base is a little less than one third of the height of the whole object. Focus is on the cottage and the front of the base; depth of field is shallow, so the surface falls soft toward the camera and everything behind the globe dissolves.

THE BASE IS NEW AND MORE SCULPTURAL. It replaces the base of images 1 and 2.
A pedestal turned and hand-carved from dark walnut with polished brass, seen straight on. From the top down: a polished brass collar gripping the glass; a broad projecting ledge of carved walnut with a brass edge; a waist carved in deep relief with holly leaves, berries and pine cones, cut into the walnut itself and left unpainted, the same dark oiled brown as the rest of the wood, with no green and no red; a wider stepped foot ringed with a brass rope moulding. Soft real snow is piled along the top of the ledge, banked against the glass, with small drifts in the carving. A row of real clear icicles of uneven length hangs from the underside of the ledge rim all the way round, the longest reaching nearly down to the foot, each one catching warm light from the globe on one side and cold blue light on the other. A little hoarfrost dulls the brass. No garland, no ribbon, no lettering on the base.

THE SETTING IS NEW.
The globe stands on one dark polished surface: a single unbroken top, nearly black, with a deep piano gloss and only the faintest wood grain, running back from the camera and dissolving into darkness behind the globe. Like a black mirror, it shows a clear, slightly soft reflection of the base, the icicles and the warm glow directly beneath the foot.
Flat fern frost has grown outward over the gloss from the foot of the base, the way frost flowers grow on a window pane: branching, feathery, crystalline fronds lying flat on the surface and radiating from the foot on every visible side, densest at the foot and fading to bare black gloss about one base-width away. It is frost that grew there, not fallen snow: no loose snow, no scattered granules, no drifts on the surface. Nothing else lies on the surface.
The background is nothing but soft out-of-focus darkness: deep blue-black, with large soft round bokeh discs of cold blue and silver-white light and a few small warm gold ones, sparse and of different sizes, far behind the globe. The air around the globe is still and completely empty: no snow falls outside the glass, no flakes, no specks, no dust, no sparkles in the air. No window, no window frame, no curtain, no wall, no furniture, no candles, no recognisable object of any kind.

PARTICLES: FAR DENSER THAN IN IMAGE 1.
Heavy, slow snowfall fills the whole volume of the liquid: many hundreds of white flakes of uneven size hanging and drifting gently down at every depth from the front glass to the back glass and from the ground to the top of the dome. Depth is unmistakable: the nearest flakes are large soft out-of-focus discs close against the front glass; the middle ones are small and crisp, some passing in front of the cottage and the tree; the farthest are tiny and faint, behind the tree and the chimney. Flakes near the string lights glow amber, those near the glass catch cold blue light. The cottage, tree, pond, lamppost, fence and path stay clearly readable through the snowfall. Every flake is inside the liquid, behind the glass. Outside the glass there is not one flake in the air.

LIGHT.
Cold blue evening light: a cool soft key from high on the right draws a long cool highlight down the upper right of the dome and a thin cold rim along its left edge, and lays a faint blue sheen on the polished surface. The dome reflects only soft blurred lights, never a window. The warmest and brightest thing in the frame is the light inside the globe: the string lights, the lamppost and the cottage windows glow amber through the glass and spill out onto the snow on the ledge, the brass, the icicles, and a soft amber pool on the frosted surface around the foot. Deep shadows, restrained colour, fine film grain.
