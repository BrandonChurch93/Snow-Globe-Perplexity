This is the heart of the piece. The owner's words: the shake has to be on point and look incredible, and the particles are things in a liquid, not confetti and not a filter.

- The liquid model (`src/globe/liquid.ts`): what section 7 of `kit/03-METHOD.md` describes: the solid turn the shake gives the liquid and its decay over `shake.spinDownSeconds`, the slosh from the strokes and the tilt, the gentle ambient drift at rest. It is a closed-form velocity field of position, of its own small stepped state and of the globe's motion: every velocity it gives lies inside the sphere and slides along the wall. At rest it stays at rest.
- The simulation (`src/particles/engine/`): one pool sized to the largest season in `config.particles`, a particle's kind decided by its index and the season, every kind's behaviour in one table from `kit/02-SUBJECT.md` section 4 (how fast it sinks or floats, how it follows the liquid, how it tumbles, flutters or glides, whether it rests, where, and how it is thrown back up). Fixed steps of `sim.stepHz`. Collisions against a signed distance grid of the miniature and the ground, given to the engine as `{n, sizeMm, data: Float32Array}` with `n` cubed values, centred on the sphere's centre, positive outside solids, plus the sphere's wall analytically; a small helper builds such a grid from simple shapes (boxes, a cone, a ball, a ground plane) for the tests and for the blockout. Coming to rest on surfaces in a way that looks settled, not stacked in a grid. Thrown back by the shake. A season change inside a shake: kinds are paired by index, the outgoing kind fades out and the incoming fades in by a per-particle number inside the swap window, with the counts of each kind in each season from the config, so nothing pops.
- The loop (section "The card loop"): closed-form closed paths for loop mode, every period dividing the loop length, every particle back where it started at the loop's end, as the tests in "Tests" ask.
- Shape: keep what `SpikeSim` gives the rest of the engine, so it can be swapped in: `reset(...)`, `advanceTo(step, timeline)`, `snapshot()` and `restore(...)` for checkpoints, typed arrays for position, a per-particle orientation or phase, kind, size and opacity, and the liquid's state. The same start and the same globe motion give the same state at step N however the steps are grouped. It must run in a worker as well as on the main thread: add a thin worker wrapper that posts the typed arrays, for the live app.
- Speed matters: the pool is about a hundred thousand particles at `sim.stepHz`. Measure a simulated second in the storm and at rest in Node, report it, and bring it down as far as you can (structure of arrays, no allocation in the step, particles at rest skipped until the liquid moves them).
- Tests as "Tests" lists them for the simulation, the liquid model and the loop.
