Read first: `kit/01-FILM.md` from "The prompt opening of film A" to the end of the file; `kit/03-METHOD.md` section 13; in `kit/05-ARCHITECTURE.md` the lines on `opening()`, `renderOpeningFrame`, film A and the local render (search for `opening`); in `kit/07-QUALITY-BAR.md` G7, G13, D3 and the judged frames of the opening; `kit/09-QA-GAUNTLET.md` items 14 and 23. ## What exists
A stand-in with the right lengths. `openingPlan()` in `src/film/opening.ts` holds the film's times by frame (the lead, a key for every character, the reasoning control opened and chosen, the send, the build screen, a frame for every line). `drawOpening()` in `src/film/openingStandIn.ts` draws a dark page of plain HTML and flags itself as a stand-in. Film A is not finished while it stands.

## What is asked
The real opening: the product's own prompt screen with the prompt typed out, the reasoning level opened and set, the send, and the build screen, drawn by the team's skill `synthetic-ui` version 2.1.0 and shown by the page frame by frame on the film's clock.

The skill is attached as `synthetic-ui-skill.tar.gz`. Unpack it to `~/.perplexity/skills/space/synthetic-ui` (the path it has in the root's VM), run `npm ci --ignore-scripts` in it, and read all of it: `SKILL.md`, `references/spec.md`, `references/sequences.md`, `scripts/composer.mjs`, `scripts/sequence.mjs`, `scripts/sequence-model.mjs`, `scripts/render.mjs`. Its pages are pure functions of time: `renderAt(seconds)` on a canvas of 1280 x 720 logical pixels at a device scale (1.5 gives 1920 x 1080, 3 gives 3840 x 2160).

Three more files are attached, from an earlier build of Brandon's where the same job was done with an older version of this skill: `kestrel-opening-RECIPE.md` (how it was done and why), `kestrel-tools-opening.ts` (the tool) and `kestrel-Opening.ts` (the page's side). They are a pattern to read, not code to copy: another repo, 30 frames a second, another version of the skill.

### 1. Nothing of the skill enters the repo
Not its code and not its typeface. The typeface is Perplexity's interface font, for private use, and this repo is public. Nothing of it goes under `public/` or `dist/` either. Only rendered pictures are kept. The tool copies the skill to `out/work/opening/skill/` and works on that copy.

### 2. The tool: `tools/opening.mjs`, run as `npm run opening`
- `--skill DIR` names the skill. Without it the tool looks in `~/.perplexity/skills/space/synthetic-ui`, then `~/.perplexity/skills/org/synthetic-query-typing`. If none is there it stops and says so in one plain line.
- It drives the skill's two pages (the composer, then the thread in `thinking` mode with no `result`) in the pinned headless browser, the way `tools/lib/page.mjs` and `tools/lib/server.mjs` start and serve pages, and draws one picture for every frame of `openingPlan()`.
- The film's clock replaces the skill's own pacing. `kit/03-METHOD.md`: "If what the skill produces runs on timers, replace its timers with the film's clock. That is the one change you make to it." So every time the skill's timeline holds (when typing starts, when each character is on screen, when the pointer moves and presses, when each line arrives and is done) comes from `openingPlan()`, and one second of the film is one second of every motion the skill draws: the pointer, the fades, the morph of the send control, the spinner. No stretch of the skill's time is squeezed or stretched, and nothing that moves in the skill steps in jumps because the typing does. If you lay film frames over the skill's own clock from outside instead, as the earlier build did, the same must hold, shown on a `seq` of the typing's first half second.
- On every frame of the typing the picture holds exactly the characters `keyTaps` puts on that frame: a key sound is laid on each of those frames.
- The edits to the work copy are exact replacements with a guard: if the skill's source no longer matches an edit, the tool stops and names the edit. The text of your edits is the build's own code and lives in the tool; it quotes no more of the skill than the short anchors it needs.
- Output, the pack: `public/opening/manifest.json` and `public/opening/opening.bin`. Pictures at 1920 x 1080, lossless, each picture stored once however many frames show it. The manifest says, for every frame, which picture it shows and what the skill's own state said when it was drawn (characters typed, the level the chip shows, the menu open or not, sent or not, lines on screen, lines done). The pack is at most 12 MB (the hosted app carries it, and `npm run hostcheck` must still pass). If whole pictures do not fit, keep them lossless and store less: for example one ground picture for each state and, for a frame, only the smallest box that differs. Do not go lossy without asking `/root`.
- `--master` draws the same frames at device scale 3 into `out/work/opening/master/` (never committed, never hosted). When that pack exists, the tools' server offers it and the page uses it whenever the surface it draws on is wider than the hd pack's pictures, so film A's opening at `formats.master` is native 3840 x 2160, not the hd pictures enlarged. Without it the page enlarges the hd pack and the tools say so in one line. Either way the chunk's record names the pack its frames came from (`master` or `hd, enlarged`), and `tools/film.mjs` prints it.
- It ends with its own checks and writes them to `out/work/opening/checks.json`: the skill's checks (`fontLoaded`, `noRuntimeErrors`, `fixedFraming`, `sequenceLayout`, `oneActiveStep`, `activeHighlightConsistent`, and that the same time asked twice gives the same picture), and the film's: the chip shows `intro.reasoningFrom` on every frame before `reasoning.choose` and `intro.reasoning` on every frame from it; the menu is open on every frame from `reasoning.open` up to `reasoning.choose` and on no other; the typed text is right on every frame and equals `intro.prompt` from the last key on; the send is pressed on `send`; line `n` is on screen from `steps[n]`; every line is done before the last frame; the last frame shows no working line. A pack is written only when every check passes.
- Drawn twice, the pack is the same bytes. Say its sha256.

### 3. The page: `src/film/openingPack.ts` in place of `src/film/openingStandIn.ts`
- `drawOpening(root, frame)` keeps its name and shape for the live app. It shows the pack's picture for that frame on one canvas that fills `#opening` (the film's formats are all 16:9; on any other shape it is fitted whole, centred, on the skill's ground colour, which the manifest holds). It never draws a wrong picture: when the picture of the asked frame is not decoded yet it keeps the last one and asks for the next few ahead.
- `renderOpeningFrame(i)` in `src/capture/showcase.ts` is exact: it waits until the pack is loaded and the picture of frame `i` is decoded and drawn, and throws with a plain message if the pack is missing or damaged. It never returns over a blank or an older picture.
- The live player of film A (`?mode=autopilot&which=a`) waits for the pack before it starts, as it waits for the seasons' files, and plays the opening at the display's rate without a dropped picture on an ordinary laptop. The pack is fetched only when film A is asked for, never by the plain app.
- `flagStandIn('opening')` goes, and with it the stand-in file. `info().standIns` holds nothing for the opening.
- Memory: decoded pictures are kept in a small window, not all at once.

### 4. The film's times: `src/film/opening.ts`
`openingPlan()` stays the one truth for every time, for the sound cues as well (`keyTaps`, `send`, the frame count). Keep every field it has; add what the tool needs (the pointer's travel, the frame the last line is done). The numbers of `intro` in `config/showcase.json` are Brandon's and fixed: 14 characters a second, 0.8 s before the send, 0.9 s between lines, 1.2 s of hold, at most 16 s. The pauses in `PAUSES` are the build's own and yours to set, by these rules:
- A lead of about half a second on the empty composer, so the first frame is the product's screen at rest.
- Every travel of the pointer takes at least half a second and reads as a hand moving, never a jump. One pointer for all three presses (the chip, the level, the send): it comes in once and stays.
- The menu stands fully open, with the pointer on its way to or on the level, for at least 0.9 s.
- The pointer goes to the send control during the 0.8 s pause, so the press lands where the pause ends.
- After the press, the skill's own beat (press, pulse, the slight fade) before the hard switch to the build screen.
- The build screen: about 0.6 s with the prompt and the title before the first line. The last line works for part of the hold and every line stands done for at least 0.35 s before the cut. The whole build screen stays near 4.5 s: Brandon wants the prompt to have the time and the build to be quick.
- The whole opening is at most `intro.maxSeconds`, and shorter is better as long as everything can be read. `kit/01-FILM.md`: "If it would be longer, shorten the pauses, never the typing speed."
Say the final numbers, frame by frame, in your report.

### 5. The reasoning level: seen, in the skill's own style
`kit/01-FILM.md`: "Before the prompt is sent, the reasoning level control is opened and `intro.reasoning` is chosen. This is seen, not implied." The skill draws the control at rest (a sparkle, the model's name, the level, a chevron) but not its menu. `kit/03-METHOD.md`: "If the reasoning level control is not part of what the skill draws, add it in the skill's own style." So you add, in the work copy only:
- The pointer presses the control (the skill's own pointer, press and pulse). A menu opens under it, right edges in line, the way the skill's own slash menu opens (its rise of 8 px, its fade, `shadowMenu`, its border, its row tint and radius, its small muted heading). It opens downward so that not one character of the typed prompt is covered, and it stays inside the 1280 x 720 frame with room to spare.
- The menu lists the levels in `intro.reasoningLevels`, one row each, in the type and colours the skill uses for a control of that size. The level in force carries a teal check at the right. The row under the pointer takes the skill's row tint.
- The pointer travels to the row of `intro.reasoning` and presses it. On that frame the check moves, the chip's label changes, and the menu goes the way the slash menu goes.
- Everything is drawn with the skill's own helpers, colours, weights and easing. Invent nothing the skill does not already have a form for. Where you had to invent a measure, say which.
- New in `config/showcase.json` under `intro`, set by `/root` (decision 135), and read from there, never written into code: `"model": "Claude Fable 5.2"` (the chip's model name: the model this build was run with, under the name the product lists it by), `"reasoningFrom": "Standard"`, `"reasoningLevels": ["Light", "Standard", "High", "Extra High", "Max"]` (the five level names the skill itself accepts, weakest first).

### 6. The build screen
The skill's thread in `thinking` mode: the product's label at the top left, the prompt in its bubble, the heading `intro.buildTitle`, one row for each line of `intro.steps` with the title only. A row arrives working (the spinner and the pale card) and is done when the next arrives; no `result`; the hard cut follows the last frame.
The skill holds at most four rows and five do not fit its page under a prompt of two lines. `kit/01-FILM.md` leaves the wording of `intro.steps` open "if the skill for the opening expects a different number of lines". `/root` has set four (decision 135), with every word of Brandon's kept: "Sculpting the globe and four bases", "Simulating snow, petals, fireflies and leaves", "Bending light through glass and water", "Choreographing the shake and scoring four seasons". Make that change in the config with the three keys above.

### 7. What a viewer must be able to do
Read every word of the prompt on the frame it appears, at 1920 x 1080 and in a 400 px wide copy of the frame as far as the type's size allows; see the level change; read each line of the build as it arrives. Make `npm run sheet -- --opening` and look at it at full size. Look at single frames at their own size: frame 0, the middle of the typing, the first frame with the whole prompt, the control pressed, the menu open with the pointer on the level, the frame after the choice, the send pressed, the first frame of the build screen, each line working, each line just done, the last frame. Look at a `seq` across each press and across the switch between the two screens, frame by frame. Nothing clipped, nothing overlapping, no flicker, no picture out of order.
