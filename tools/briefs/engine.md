This is Phase 2: the engine every later phase builds on, with every part as a stand-in. The bar of the finished piece is a real object filmed with a macro lens. Nothing you draw now is judged on realism, so spend nothing on looks. What must be right now is the plumbing, because the look lock starts from it: the passes, the refraction, the mirror, the layout, the change, the collider, the stand-in flags, the diagnostic views and determinism.

What to build:

- The stage. `createStage` in `src/scene/createStage.ts` returns your stage, which implements `Stage` in `src/core/stage.ts`. One scene graph for every mode, at real size in millimetres from `globe`: the surface is the plane y = 0, the foot of the base stands on it at the origin. `drawSample(target, pose, sample, moment, shadows)` draws one sample: `sample` carries the pixel jitter, the lens point and the moment in the shutter (it is `null` in the live app, which draws one sample a frame). `moment` carries the season, `from`, `to`, the change number, the seconds since the landing, the globe's pose from the shake table, `time` for closed-form motion, `beat` inside a shake or `null`, and `period`, the loop's length when everything must repeat with a period that divides it, else `null`.
- Refraction, as decision 18 says. `src/globe/lens.ts` holds one GLSL chunk used by every material inside the liquid: for a vertex inside the sphere and this sample's point on the lens, find by fixed-step bisection the point on the sphere where the ray from the lens enters so that Snell's law holds with `globe.liquidIor`, and place the vertex along that ray at its optical path length, so depth still orders the inside. The inside is drawn in its own pass, clipped to the sphere. The shell is drawn over it: a reflection of the lights around it, a Fresnel rim, the small air bubble at the crown that lags with the liquid, and the room behind the globe seen through it, upside down and small around the miniature's edges, computed analytically. A unit test in `tests/lens.test.ts` proves the chunk's maths in TypeScript against a ray traced through the sphere: for points across the inside the projected direction agrees within a small angle.
- The surface with its mirror image, as decision 21 says: the scene drawn again from the camera reflected in y = 0, with the same shaders, honouring `StageQuality.surfaceReflection` (`planar`, or `baked`, which for now only tints). The backdrop: `scene.bokehLights` small emitters at different depths in the season's colours, real geometry in computed frames so the lens samples turn them into discs, sprites sized by their blur circle when `sample` is `null`.
- The inside light, as decision 21 says: a coarse grid of light baked on the CPU for each season from that season's inner lights, sampled by every material inside. The room light: one rig per season, read from the season's folder, crossing from one to the next with the change number.
- Materials through one helper, as decision 19 says (`src/core/materials.ts`): every part gets the projection, the light grid, the dissolve by hashed coverage and the diagnostic views from one place, so a season's folder never touches shader plumbing.
- The layout, in `src/scene/layout.ts`, shared by all four seasons, exactly as the table in `kit/02-SUBJECT.md` section 2 words it: the cottage left of centre and turned a little toward the viewer, about a quarter of the dome's height; the tree right of centre, the tallest thing, its crown reaching about three quarters of the dome's height; the lamppost between them and a little forward; the pond front and centre at the island's edge; the fence front left; the path from the door toward the pond; the island a low mound, higher at the back, its top at `globe.islandLevelMm`. The same shapes give the collider: `collider(season)` returns an `SdfGrid` (`n` cubed values, centred on the sphere's centre, positive outside solids, millimetres), built once for each season. `tests/layout.test.ts` checks that everything lies inside the glass and that the heights hold.
- A season is one folder, as decision 20 says: `src/seasons/<season>/index.ts` exports one kit (give the type its own file): the base, what covers the miniature, the glass dressing, the surface dressing, the light rig and inner lights, the veil, the arrival, the backdrop's colours and the colours of its particle kinds. All four exist, as stand-ins, so three agents can each take one folder in Phase 4 without touching anything shared.
- Stand-ins for every part in the table of "The parts register", as step 2 of Phase 2 words them: each base a turned shape in its season's main colour, the miniature as boxes, a cone and a ball in the fixed layout, each particle kind as plain points in its colour, each dressing as a flat tint, each veil as a tinted fog filling the sphere whose strength follows the liquid's turbulence (`ParticleSource.liquid`), each arrival as its finished state faded in over `shake.settleCheckSeconds`. Every stand-in says so itself: its object carries `userData.standIn` with its part's name, and code paths call `flagStandIn`. `npm run parts` writes `parts/PARTS.json` with every part as `stand-in` and the hash of its files: bring the file lists in `tools/parts.mjs` in line with the folders as they now are.
- The particles' drawing, in `src/particles/draw/`, from a `ParticleSource`: `kinds[kindOf[i]]` names a particle's kind, its place is between `prev` and `pos` by `moment.mix`, `opacity` and `phase` are used when present, coverage is hashed so nothing is sorted, and with `sample` null each particle is stretched along its velocity. Drawn through the lens chunk like everything inside. `setQuality` draws only the first share by index. Read the arrays from the source at every draw and keep no reference to them between draws: the source may hand out different arrays from one draw to the next (the stepped simulation in the film and the app, closed paths in the card loop). The simulation itself is another agent's: do not write one.
- The change. Every seasonal part reads `moment.change` with `moment.from` and `moment.to`. `partChange(part, change)` in `src/shake/change.ts` says how far each part has gone, 0 before the window, 1 after it, never outside 0 to 1: inside the glass the far side of the miniature first and the near side after; outside the glass a plain dissolve under a short bloom of the new season's inner light at the fastest stroke. All four changes switch the stand-ins.
- The diagnostic views of `kit/04-LOOK.md` section 9, all eleven, with the encodings `tools/README.md` promises to the other tools (`ids`, `standins`, `cover`, `texels`, `change`); choose and document the rest by adding rows to that table in your report, not in the file. A diagnostic picture has one sample and no lens blur.
- The post chain in `src/post/`: keep `Post`'s interface (`exposure`, `seed`, `resolve(mean, raw)`). Add the glow, the grain and the faint corner darkening in their simplest form, after accumulation, deterministic.
