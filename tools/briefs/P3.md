# Phase 3, the look lock: what every builder of a part shares

This comes after `tools/briefs/COMMON.md`, which still holds, and before your own brief. Where they differ, your own brief wins, then this file, then COMMON.

## The bar

The finished piece must be taken for a real snow globe filmed with a macro lens in a dark room. You build one part of winter to that bar. Stand-ins are over: a part that ships as a flat tint, a primitive shape, a plain point or a plain gradient fails.

- The targets are `references/approved/winter-rest.png` and `references/approved/winter-shake.png`, 2816 by 1584. Look at them at full size around your part before you write a line, and again before every push.
- Match the approved image first. Where the kit's words and the picture differ, the picture wins, unless a `settled` line says otherwise (`keys[].settled` and `settledAll` in the config).
- Read, every line: all of `kit/04-LOOK.md` and `kit/06-ASSETS.md`; in `kit/07-QUALITY-BAR.md` the critics R1, R3 and R4 with their tells, and the measures your brief names; lines 135 to 149 and 225 to 239 of `references/drafts/round2/NOTES.md`; in `kit/PROGRESS.md` the section "Look lock" and decisions 36 to 41; then the kit sections your brief names. Read a section again right before you build what it governs.
- R3's tells are what a critic will look for: a hard round highlight, no rim thickness, an inside that is not enlarged, a sphere ending on a hard line at the base, flat round particles, a plastic base, a mirror image as sharp far away as at contact. None may be true of your part.
- Every surface has height as well as colour. Nothing is lit by a constant. Nothing is perfectly clean, perfectly regular or perfectly symmetric.
- Do not lower the bar to finish sooner. If a method fails, go down the fallback chain below and say so.

## Frames and sizes

Millimetres everywhere. Read sizes from `globe` in `config/showcase.json` and from `src/scene/layout.ts`, never from memory.

- World: the slab is the plane y = 0, the globe's axis is x = 0, z = 0, +y is up, +z points at the `hero` camera, +x is right as `hero` sees it.
- Base frame: the group `globe`, which is the world frame at rest and moves and tilts in a shake. The base's foot centre is its origin. `kit.base()` is a child of it.
- Inside frame: the group `inside`, origin at the sphere's centre (`centreHeightMm` above the slab), same axes. The miniature, the arrival, the lights and the glass dressing are children of it. `groundY` is the island's level and `collarY` the collar's rim in this frame; `groundAt(x, z)` is the ground's height.
- Everything inside the liquid is drawn through the lens: `stageMaterial({ inside: true })` places each vertex where the eye sees it through the refracting sphere (`src/globe/lens.ts`). So the inside looks enlarged and squeezed toward the rim, as in the approved image. Model at real size and let the lens do it.

## The reference camera and the fit tool

The approved winter image was measured: the camera that drew it is `REF_RIG` in `tools/lib/refcam.mjs`. With it the glass, the collar and the foot of this build fall on the approved image's own edges, the foot about 2 mm high, which is accepted.

- `npm run fit -- --tier draft` draws the build with that camera and writes `out/work/fit/winter-pair.jpg` (approved above, build below), `winter-blend.jpg` (half and half) and `winter-lines.jpg` (the approved image's edges in yellow over the build), and prints the mean difference. `--box x0,y0,x1,y1` in pixels of the approved image compares one region, `--name N` names the files, `--wide 1600` sets their width, `--force` draws again. For the storm, draw `npm run still -- --season winter --state landed --camera hero --tier draft` and hold it beside `winter-shake.png`.
- `tools/lib/refcam.mjs` for scripts: `projectWorld([x, y, z])` gives the pixel of a world point outside the glass; `projectInside([x, y, z])` gives the pixel where a point in the inside frame is seen through the lens; `rayOf(px, py)` and `insideRayOf(px, py)` give the ray of a pixel in the world and, bent by the glass, inside the liquid (origin and direction in the inside frame). Use them to measure where things stand in the approved image and to project it onto geometry.
- Other pictures: `npm run still -- --season winter --state rest|landed|settled --camera hero|macro|interior|base|surface|low --tier draft --format preview` (add `--diag standins|ids|cover|texels|change|...` for a diagnostic view, `--rig '<json>'` for a camera of your own). `tools/README.md` lists every command and view. A `draft` picture at `preview` size is for looking; check your part once at `--format hd` before you report.

## One frame object, one kit

- `src/scene/frame.ts` holds `StageFrame`, filled once a frame by the stage and handed to every module: the size, the camera `pose`, `sample`, `time`, `period`, `arrived`, the two seasons `from` and `to` with `change` and `mix`, the light (`keyDir`, `keyCol`, `rimDir`, `rimCol`, `sky`, `ground`, `fill`, `ambient`, `pool`, `grids`), the liquid's state numbers `liquid` with `turbulence`, the globe's pose `globe` (x, y, z in mm, `tiltX`, `tiltZ` in radians) with `globeUp`, `quality`, `planar`, `focalPx`, `blurMm`, and `kinds`.
- Everything outside the glass answers the globe's pose from that object: the mirror image, the shadow, the glow on the slab, the dressing around the foot.
- `src/seasons/kit.ts` holds `SeasonKit`. `src/seasons/winter/index.ts` composes winter from one file per part; each of those files exports `prepare()` and `update(moment: KitMoment)` and what its part needs. `KitMoment` has `time`, `period`, `sinceLanding` (seconds since the globe landed, `Infinity` when no landing is near), `arrived` (0 at the landing to 1 when the arrival is over), `beat`, `seed` and `frame`. An arrival is a pure function of `sinceLanding`; nothing keeps state from frame to frame.
- Materials come from `src/core/materials.ts` only: `stageMaterial({ part, standIn: false, inside, miniature, color, vertexColors, roughness, metalness, emissive, map, side, physical, extra, hook, key, change })` for lit surfaces and `stageShader({ part, standIn: false, vertexPars, vertexMain, fragment, uniforms, params })` for your own shader. They add the lens, the light grid, the veil, the change and the diagnostic views. `hook` gives you the compiled shader for your own chunks (height, wetness, sparkle).
- The part names are `glass`, `liquid`, `surface`, `backdrop`, `lens`, `shake`, `particle-engine`, `light-engine`, `base-winter`, `miniature-winter`, `glass-dressing-winter`, `surface-dressing-winter`, `light-winter`, `veil-winter`, `arrival-winter`, `particle-flake`, `particle-glitter`.
- A real part passes `standIn: false` on every material, removes its `flagStandIn(...)` call or its entry in `standIns`, and says in its report which state it reached. `npm run still -- ... --diag standins` must show your part in its own colour, not flagged.
- The room no one sees is analytic, in `src/scene/room.ts`: `vec3 stageRoom(vec3 d)` is its light from direction `d` (walls, floor, the big soft source of the key light), `vec3 stageRoomLights(vec3 d, float soft)` the backdrop's small emitters, `vec3 stageRoomSoft(vec3 d, float rough)` the room blurred for a rough surface. Include `ROOM_GLSL` and spread `roomUniforms` to use them. Those three names and meanings are fixed; their bodies belong to the builder of the scene.
- What has come to rest builds up. The particle engine may fill `ParticleSource.cover` (`src/core/stage.ts`): the thickness in millimetres of what lies above each cell of an `n` by `n` map over x and z of the inside frame, `spanMm` wide and centred on the axis, `mm[iz * n + ix]`. The stage uploads it each frame. Any material may read it through `src/core/chunks/cover.ts`: include `COVER_GLSL`, spread `coverUniforms`, and `float stageCover(vec2 xz)` gives the thickness at a place in the inside frame, 0 when nothing lies there or no map exists yet.
- Render orders in use: backdrop dome -2, slab -3, through view -0.5, surface dressing 2, bubble 4, particles 5, stamp 6, shell 10, backdrop lights 20. The glass dressing draws after the shell.
- Random numbers come from `stream(name)` in `src/core/rng.ts`. The same seed gives the same picture, always: `npm run determinism` must pass.

## Assets

- Geometry and maps are built by scripts, never by hand: `assets-src/<part>/build.mjs`, run with `npm run make -- <part>`, with three in Node and `tools/lib/glb.mjs` (`fromThree(name, geometry, extras)`, `writeGlb(file, meshes, extras)`). It writes into `assets-full/<group>/`, where `<group>` is `winter` or `shared`: models as `.glb`, pictures as PNG named `<name>.<srgb|lin>[.tile][.wNNN].png` (`srgb` for colour, `lin` for height, normals and masks, `tile` for a repeating map, `wNNN` to cap the web copy's long edge). The `full` set is lossless, at most `assetSets.full` sizes.
- `npm run texpack` makes the `web` copies in `public/assets/` and the index; `npm run build` after it. On the page: `prepareModel(path)` and `geometry(path, name)` from `src/core/models.ts`, `texture(path)` from `src/core/textures.ts`, with `path` as `<group>/<file>`; a group's files are fetched before your `prepare()` runs. Stills use the `web` set unless you pass `--assets full`.
- Commit the scripts, their inputs and the built files in `assets-full/`. No file over `repo.maxFileMb`. Keep to your own file names inside the group folders; your brief gives your prefix.
- Every generated or built asset gets a recipe in `assets-src/<part>/RECIPE.md` in the form of `kit/06-ASSETS.md` ("Recipes"), and the scripts with the committed inputs must make every shipped file again. In a recipe the tool is named in full; that is the one place a model name is written.
- At most `assets.maxTakesPerAsset` takes of one generated picture and `assets.maxTuningLooksPerPart` looks while tuning one part. Count them and report the counts.

## Generated pictures

- You can generate and edit pictures yourself with the image tool of this product, and look at them. For everything new that ships use `nano_banana_pro`, `gpt_image_2_6_flare` or `gpt_image_2_6_sunburst`. `seedream_5` only where a picture has to match the approved image closely, and say so in the recipe.
- Always pass the approved winter image, or your crop of it, as a reference, and say in the prompt what must stay the same. No person, hand, text or logo in any picture.
- What generation is for: material sheets (flat, evenly lit, no shadows, tileable where needed), views of your part from other sides, height pictures, patterns. A height picture from a generator is a guess: check it, or derive height from the colour sheet with your own filter.
- The approved image may be projected onto the faces that look at `hero` as a starting texture, corrected for the lens (`insideRayOf`, `projectInside`) and with its light taken out (divide by a blurred copy, lift the shadows, remove the highlights), then extended round the sides by generation or by your own synthesis. Remove the floating flakes first: mask the small bright blobs and inpaint them, so the alignment holds.
- No pixel of an approved image is ever drawn in a frame as it is. A projected and delit texture on real geometry, lit by the build's own light, is the method; a pasted picture is not.
- There is no tool here that turns a picture into a 3D model. Geometry is yours to write.

## The fallback chain

Three tries a step, then one step down, and say which state the part reached:

1. `built`: the method `kit/03-METHOD.md` names for the part.
2. `fallback`: the fallback the kit names for that part.
3. `general fallback`: plain geometry with generated maps for colour, height and roughness.
4. `last resort`: the region of the approved image projected onto a simple shape and lit.

## Budgets

The whole globe has to run live on a laptop. Guide numbers for winter, to be measured at integration with `npm run budget`: draw calls about 10 for the miniature, 6 for the base, 5 for the glass, 4 for the particles, 6 for the scene and 4 for the dressing; about 190000 static triangles in one pass (the scene is drawn again for the mirror image and for the shadow); texture memory about 90 to 100 MB for a season in the `web` set. Merge geometry by material. Report your part's triangles, draw calls and texture bytes.

## Rhythm

- Brandon watches through statuses with pictures every half hour, and they are made from your looks branch. Push your first look within 30 minutes of starting, then one with every heartbeat: your part in the `hero` frame beside the same region of the approved image (`npm run fit -- --box ...` does that), and a close view. Each `captions.txt` line says in plain words what the picture shows and what is still wrong.
- Send `/root` a first report as soon as your part is real, its tests are clean and it is pushed; aim for 90 minutes. Then keep raising it against the approved image, push as you go, and send a final report when it stops getting better or at the hard limit of `budget.buildAgentMaxHours`.
- A report holds, besides what COMMON asks: the state reached, the counts above, the takes and looks used, every number you measured from the approved image, each thing a critic could still fault, and one numbered recipe for making this part again for another season (steps with commands), since spring, summer and fall are built from it.
