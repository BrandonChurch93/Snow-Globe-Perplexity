# The harness and the page

What the tools in this folder can rely on. The page is opened through `tools/lib/page.mjs` (`Session`), and everything is asked for through `window.showcase`, the frame interface of `kit/05-ARCHITECTURE.md`. `src/capture/showcase.ts` holds its types.

## Extra calls under `window.showcase.dev`

| Call | Gives |
| --- | --- |
| `parts()` | `{name, color}` for every part: the flat colour that part has in the `ids` and `standins` views, as `0xRRGGBB` |
| `pose(camera)` | `[px, py, pz, ax, ay, az, fovDeg]` in millimetres: the same seven numbers `filmState(i).camera` holds |
| `framing(camera)` | The framing numbers of `kit/02-SUBJECT.md` section 9 for the current format |
| `stillFraming()` | The same numbers for the camera that drew the last `renderStill`. A screen still (`interface` or `live`, from `hero`) is drawn from the app's own framing, which stands back from `hero` to leave room for the controls and for a shake, so its numbers are not `framing('hero')`. `renderStill` in `tools/lib/page.mjs` writes these into the record |
| `judged()` | The computed judged list: `{id, unit, kind, format, what, ...}`. `kind` is `key`, `still`, `change`, `live`, `film`, `opening` or `loop` |
| `testShake()` | Frame numbers of the test shake, winter into winter, at `film.fps`: `{frameCount, first, liftEnd, strokesEnd, landing, swap: {first, last}}` |
| `testShakeState(i)`, `loopState(i)` | The same object as `filmState(i)`, for the test shake and for the card loop |
| `renderTestShakeFrame(i, tier)` | Frame `i` of the test shake, from `hero` |
| `renderShakeMoment(shot, at, tier)` | One moment of a shake, always from `hero`. `shot` is a shake's id in `film.shots`, or `test`. `at` is `swap` (the middle of the swap window), `landing`, `landed` (`states.landedSeconds` later) or `settle` (`shake.settleCheckSeconds` after the landing) |
| `loopPlan()` | `{frameCount, shake: {first, liftEnd, strokesEnd, landing}}` at `film.fps` |
| `openingPlan()` | The opening of film A by frame: `{fps, frameCount, seconds, typing, reasoning, send, build, steps, keyTaps, pointer, menu, lastDone, pacing}`. `reasoning` is `{first, open, choose, last}`: the pointer comes in, the control is pressed and the menu opens, the level is pressed, the last frame before the pointer is gone. `pointer` is `{enter, atControl, toLevel, atLevel, gone, toSend, atSend}`, `menu` is `{full, fade, gone}`, `lastDone` is the frame the last line is done, `pacing` holds the seconds of each part of the level change |
| `sanity()` | After a picture: `{invalid, pixels}`, the count of invalid or infinite values in the picture before lens and grade |
| `inside()` | After a picture: `{inside, outside, total}`, particles inside a solid and outside the sphere in that picture's state |
| `simcache(what)` | Runs the simulation along `film`, `stills` or `loop` and stores its checkpoints. Gives `{checkpoints, simulatedSeconds, ms}` |
| `timings()`, `caps()`, `simulate(from, seconds)` | Timing of the last pictures, the WebGL limits in use, the cost of simulated time |

## Diagnostic views

Set with `setDiagnostic(view)` before a render call, `none` to clear. A diagnostic picture is drawn with one sample and no lens, tone curve, grain or dither, so its values can be read back exactly.

| View | Encoding |
| --- | --- |
| `ids` | Each pixel is the flat colour of its part, from `dev.parts()` |
| `standins` | A pixel of a stand-in is that part's colour. Everything else is black |
| `cover` | Red: how much of the miniature is hidden at that pixel, 0 to 1. Blue: 1 where the pixel shows the miniature, 0 elsewhere |
| `texels` | Red: texels per screen pixel divided by 4, clamped. Green: the blur circle in pixels divided by 8, clamped. Blue: 1 where the pixel is a textured pixel of a base or of the miniature |
| `change` | Red: how far the part at that pixel is through its change |

## Signals the page gives

- `document.documentElement.dataset.firstPicture` is `1` once the first complete picture of the default season is on screen in `live`.
- `document.documentElement.dataset.audio` is `none` until the first interaction, `started` once an audio context exists.
- The stage is `#app`: `canvas#view`, then `div#opening`, then `div#ui`. `Session.grabStage(page)` captures that region with the interface. `grab` captures the canvas alone.
- Controls carry `data-control`: `shake`, `season` (with `data-season`), `sound`. The menu on the `M` key is `[data-menu]`.
- `document.documentElement.dataset.detail` is `loading` from the first picture until every whole picture has replaced its first file, then `ready`.
- `#opening` carries `data-pack` once a picture of the opening is on it: `hd` (the committed pack at its own size), `master` (the pack of `npm run opening -- --master`) or `hd, enlarged` (a surface wider than the hd pack and no master pack).

The crops are cut with reduced motion asked for, so no transition runs, and a crop is kept only when two shots in a row are the same. The four season marks are labeled mark 1 to mark 4 by their place on the screen, and which is which is told only in the key. They are kept with records in `out/work/controls/`.
- `gate`: `--phase N [--only ID,ID] [--unit ID] [--fresh]`. `--unit` names the unit of the round for G13. `--fresh` draws the G5 and G13 pictures again.

- `film`: `--which b|a [--tier T] [--format F] [--shot ID | --first N --last N] [--join] [--mix FILE] [--review] [--candidate NAME] [--from-hd] [--look no]`. A ranged run only renders unless `--join` is added. A join that does not cover the whole film is named `-partial` and is never kept or shown as the film. `loop`: the same range flags; writes the loop, its web copy and the card still under `out/work/loop/`.
- `stress`: `[--tier T] [--shot ID]`. Writes `out/work/stress/stress.json` with, for each moment, the frame before encoding, the encodes at `export.bitrateMbps` and `quality.compressionStressMbps`, and stills taken back out of each.
- `render-local`: `--what film-b|film-a|loop --test | --go`, or `--proof`. `--rehearse` runs `--test` or `--go` small (preview, draft, software graphics) into `out/work/local/rehearsal/`. It refuses a master when the installed Chrome is missing or draws with software, unless `deliver.sandboxMaster` is true.
- `Session.open` closes its server and drops the render lock when the browser cannot start.
- `texpack`: `[--group shared,winter] [--fresh] [--table]`. The only way a file reaches `public/assets/`; the rules are in `tools/lib/webset.mjs` and in `assets-src/web-pack/RECIPE.md`. A picture becomes `name.webp` (WebP near-lossless, no channel of any pixel off by more than 2 of 255, checked as it is written) and, when it is opaque and 1024 px or more, `name.first.webp` at half the size; a picture with a side under 128 px stays `.png`. A `.glb` gets a lighter list of triangles for `medium` and `low` and is packed with `EXT_meshopt_compression`. Files the full set no longer names are removed. `--table` prints every file with its sizes, coding, largest change and triangles per level; `out/work/texpack/report.json` holds the same. `--fresh` ignores the cache in `out/work/texpack/cache/`.
- `firstlook`: `[--camera hero,macro] [--season S] [--state S] [--tier T] [--stills | --live] [--wait SECONDS]`. Stills: the same still from the web set held at its first files and whole, and how far apart they are. Live: what the built page fetched before and after the first complete picture, whether every whole file arrived, and the longest wait between two frames. Writes `out/work/firstlook/`.
- `node tools/visit.mjs --url http://localhost:4173/ --layout hd|phone|tablet [--quality low|none] [--only 1,2,7] [--name NAME] [--wait SECONDS]`: the visitor's pass through the served app on one layout (Phase 5, step 2), with real pointer, touch and key events: first paint, the Shake control, the space bar, a stir, a drag around the globe, each season mark, the sound control, the keyboard and the `M` menu, reduced motion, each season by its link, the look and places of the controls. Writes a screenshot and a line of facts after every action to `out/work/visit/<name>/` (`facts.jsonl`, `result.json`); lines with `ok: false` are what it found. Serve `dist/` first (`npx vite preview --port 4173 --strictPort`). Without a GPU run one layout at a time: a full walk has nine changes of season and takes 15 to 40 minutes.
- `still --live low|medium|high` draws through the app's path at that level, and the meshes of the web set are drawn from that level's list of triangles. Without `--live` a still draws the full meshes.
- The page takes `detail=first` in its address to stay at the first files (what `firstlook` compares). The asset index is fetched with `cache: 'no-cache'` and every asset by `name?v=<hash>`, so a file that changed is never served from an old cache.
- `sheet --app shake|stir|drag|jump [--level high] [--from SEASON] [--to SEASON] [--part ID] [--stride K | --fit]`: a script of the live app (`src/app/replay.ts`) as sheets of frames drawn through the app's own painter with `showcase.dev.renderAppFrame`, at a level of `live.quality` (`high` unless asked, the judged level of the globe) and in `formats.preview` unless `--format` is given. The frames are chosen in `tools/lib/appsheets.mjs`. A shake: one sheet across the whole script, three from the press to the landing, one of the first second after the landing. A jump: two from the press to the landing and one of the first second after it. A stir and a drag: one sheet each from the pointer going down to the end, the stir's close together at first and then further apart. `--part whole|bar|landing|turn` draws one part, `--stride K` or `--fit` the whole script in consecutive frames. Each tile is labelled with its frame, its time and the season (the camera's yaw in a drag); the sheet's record holds the plan, the marks with their frames and the legend the pack gives a critic. Frames are kept in `out/work/seq/app-<script>-<seasons>-<level>-<format>/` and drawn in rising order, because the page replays the script from its start for an earlier frame. The `globe` packs of R4 and R5 call it, one command per script, so the scripts can be drawn in different VMs: a sheet whose record matches the engine, the layout and the plan is kept even when its frames are not on disk, so only `out/work/sheets/app-<script>-*` has to be carried to the VM that builds the pack. `--plain`, with `--app` or `--seasons`, makes a second sheet with `plain` in its name whose labels and legend name no season (frame and time, or the tile's place 1 to 4), for the R5 critic who has to name the seasons from the pictures; the record's spec keeps the truth and the pack writes it to the key file only.
- `strict`: `[--keys ID,ID] [--modes pow,all+smooth,all+smooth+noguard] [--format F] [--tier T] [--fresh]`. The strict rule for shader arithmetic (`tools/lib/strict.mjs`, decision 133). The software renderer answers with an ordinary number where GLSL leaves the answer open (pow with a base below zero, the square root of a number below zero, normalize of a vector of no length); some graphics cards answer with no number, which can blank a part or the whole glass. `strict` renders each judged key plainly and with every open case answering no number, and counts the pixels that differ. It passes when no pixel moves by more than one level and at most 0.01 percent of pixels move (a wrapped call is compiled a little differently, which can move a last digit). Writes `out/work/strict/report.json`. Run it after any change to a shader; `tests/glsl-safe.test.ts` holds the part of the rule that can be read from the source.
- `opening`: `[--skill DIR] [--effort DIR] [--master] [--stills N,N --only-stills] [--page]`. Draws film A's opening with the team's skill for the product's interface and writes the pack to `public/opening/` (and to `dist/opening/` when `dist/` exists). `--skill` names the skill; without it the tool looks in `~/.perplexity/skills/space/synthetic-ui`, then `~/.perplexity/skills/org/synthetic-query-typing`. `--effort` names the package that holds the change of the reasoning level; without it, `~/.perplexity/skills/org/synthetic-query-typing`. When a package is not on the machine the tool stops with one plain line and the committed pack stays as it is. `--master` draws the same frames at `formats.master` into `out/work/opening/master/`; the tools' server offers that pack at `/opening-master/` and says at `/__opening` whether it has one, and the page takes it for a surface wider than the hd pack. Without it the page enlarges the hd pack, and `film` says which pack a chunk came from, in one line. `--stills` keeps the named frames as PNG in `out/work/opening/stills/`; with `--only-stills` no pack is made. `--page` asks the built page for frames out of order and in a run and compares each with the pack, pixel for pixel (`out/work/opening/page.json`). A pack is written only when every check in `out/work/opening/checks.json` passes. Nothing of the skill, neither its code nor its typeface, is in the repo or under `public/`.
- `opening` draws with SwiftShader only: every run that draws (the pack, `--master`, `--stills`) starts its browser with SwiftShader whatever the machine's default or `SHOWCASE_RENDERER` says, says so in one line, and stops before it draws if the browser answers with another renderer. The committed pack is SwiftShader's bytes, and under lavapipe the 2D canvas the opening is painted on reads back empty and garbled frames (17 of 867 empty in each of two runs, measured 2026-11-26). `--page` draws nothing and keeps the machine's renderer. `checks.json` and `checks-master.json` name the renderer that drew (`renderer`, `graphics`), `page.json` the one that showed the pack.
- `film --fps N`: the frames a second of the run, in place of `film.previewFps`; `film.fps` must be a whole multiple of it. `npm run film -- --which a --shot opening --format hd --tier draft --fps 60 --join` gives the opening at the film's own rate, every frame of it a picture of the pack.
- `sheet --opening --first N --last M`: every frame of film A's opening from N to M, twelve to a sheet, in place of the frames across it. A sheet of the opening always draws its frames again: a frame kept under `out/work/seq/` is matched by the engine's hash, which does not cover `public/`, so it could be from an older pack.

## The master, in ranges across many VMs (`npm run master`)

The final 3840 x 2160, 60 frames a second films are rendered as chunks of `deliver.master.chunkFrames` frames. Chunk k is frames k x 30 to k x 30 + 29 of its timeline on every VM, the last one shorter. Three timelines: `film-b` (film B), `loop` (the portfolio loop) and `opening` (the opening of film A, encoded from its pack, without the engine).

Two encodes, on two kinds of machine. A render machine turns each chunk's PNGs into a near-lossless INTERMEDIATE (`deliver.master.intermediate`: x264, yuv420p, crf 6, preset medium, closed, no rate cap) and deletes the PNGs once the intermediate is checked against them. The intermediates are the archive of the render: another final encode later costs no render. The joining machine decodes all intermediates of a timeline in order and encodes each master ONCE, whole (`deliver.master.final`: two passes to a size, a key frame every 300 frames at most, scene cuts allowed), so no cap and no key frame pulses twice a second. A chunk is never a piece of the master.

The picture does not travel through the repo. An intermediate is 25 to 27 MB, the films' history would not bear 3 GB of them, and a message attachment may be 50 MiB at most, 10 to a message. So the render agent sends each chunk's `.mp4` and its `.json` record to the joining agent as message attachments, and the tool splits any file over `deliver.master.intermediate.partMb` (45) into numbered parts that `--collect` puts together again. `--ship BRANCH` carries only the record and the look (small), for an overview of what is finished.

### A render agent

It needs a built clone of the ONE commit of `main` that `/root` names (the build hash takes in `src/` and `config/`, and `--encode` refuses chunks of two builds), `npm ci`, ffmpeg, the pinned browser, and `git config user.name` and `user.email` set in the clone. Then, with the range `/root` gave it and a branch of its own that matches `deliver.master.chunkBranches`:

```
sudo apt-get install -y mesa-vulkan-drivers
npm ci
PLAYWRIGHT_HOST_PLATFORM_OVERRIDE=ubuntu24.04-x64 npx playwright-core install chromium
npm run build
npm run master -- --calibrate
npm run master -- --what film-b --first 600 --last 899 --ship agent/p6-chunks-vm07
```

`--calibrate` draws one fixed picture (the winter still at rest from the hero camera, `hd`, the review tier) and prints `calibration <sha256 of its PNG>`. It is kept in `out/work/master/calibration.json` for this machine (name, processor), this build and this renderer. No range of the film or the loop starts without it, every chunk's record carries it and the name of its renderer (`tools/lib/browser.mjs`), and a machine that prints another line than the others is not given a range: its frames could not be joined to theirs.

What it sees: one line that says how many chunks are finished and how many frames are left, then one heartbeat line a frame (chunk, frame, seconds for the frame, frames left in the range, hours left at the running average), then for each chunk `finished film-b-000600-000629: 30 frames, ... s a frame, crf 6, ... MB, luma PSNR against the PNGs least ... dB (frame ...), mean ... dB. Its PNGs are deleted`. The last lines say the range is finished and list what to send: `to send to the joining agent by attachment: N files, ... MB, none over 45 MB, in M messages of at most 10:` and then one line a message with its files. The agent sends exactly those files, as attachments, in those groups, to the joining agent. Any other ending is an error with its reason, and the exit code is not 0.

How it resumes: run the same command again, as often as needed. A finished frame is a PNG under `out/work/master/<what>/frames-<first>-<last>/` (written under another name and renamed, with its seconds and the sha256 of its pixels beside it) and is never rendered twice. A finished chunk is `out/master/<what>/<what>-<first>-<last>.mp4` with its `.json` and its look `.jpg`; it is taken as finished only when its record is from this build, this renderer, this calibration, these samples, this format and these encoder arguments (but for the crf, see below), and the mp4 and its parts still have the sha256 the record names. Chunks are never deleted by the tool. Before each chunk the tool checks `sandbox.minFreeDiskGb`, and stops without dropping anything when it is under.

The check of an intermediate, before its PNGs go: it decodes without an error to its number of frames, 1/60 s apart; EVERY decoded frame is measured against the yuv420p picture its PNG makes, on BT.709 luma, and the least must reach `deliver.master.intermediate.minPsnr` (46 dB); no frame is flat; no frame's mean luma is more than 1 code value from its PNG's. A chunk that misses only the floor is encoded again from its PNGs at a crf 2 lower, down to 0, and the record's `verify.tried` lists each try. A chunk that fails anything else keeps its PNGs and ends the run with an error. The record carries `verify.psnr` (least, mean, the least frame, and every frame's value), `verify.flatFrames`, `verify.worstMeanStep`, `sha256`, `bytes`, and `parts` (file, bytes, sha256 each) when it was split. The loop's frame 0 is the screenshot: it is kept as `out/master/loop/frame-000000.png`, named in the record with its sha256, and travels as it is with the first chunk.

- A range that is not on the chunk lines is refused, with the nearest ranges that are. No range means the whole timeline.
- `--ship BRANCH` commits each verified chunk's record and look to the orphan branch BRANCH through a clone of its own under `out/work/master/ship/`, never through the repo's `.git`, and pushes without force. A refused push is tried 4 times, 5, 15 and 30 s apart (the sign-in of a machine comes and goes), or at once when the remote was only ahead and has been merged in; then the render goes on and the push is tried again after the next chunk. The run's `shipped ...` line says at which try a push went through when it was not the first. The look at the remote and the fetch at the start of a run, and those of `--collect`, wait the same way. Git is started with `GIT_TERMINAL_PROMPT=0`, so it never stands at a prompt, and without any of `GIT_AUTHOR_NAME`, `GIT_AUTHOR_EMAIL`, `GIT_COMMITTER_NAME`, `GIT_COMMITTER_EMAIL` that is set and empty (such a one wins over the clone's `user.name` and makes the commit die); one that holds a value is left alone. The picture is not on the branch.
- `--procs P` runs P render processes side by side, each with its own browser, chunk i of the range in process i mod P. The checkpoints are made first (`npm run simcache`), so that no two processes write the same one. The default is 1. The parent sweeps the frame folders once before it starts them; after that a process reads and drops the folders of its own chunks only.
- The first line of every run is `limits: ... GiB of memory, ... CPUs`: what the process may really use. A pod can show its node's memory and cores and be held far under them, so the tool reads `memory.max` and `cpu.max` of the cgroup named in `/proc/self/cgroup` (and of every folder above it, the least), and takes the machine's own numbers only where those say `max` or are not there. Every chunk's record carries them as `limits`.
- `--procs` is refused at `uhd` when the memory cannot hold it: one `uhd` render at 24 samples holds about 4.1 GB, and 2 GB are kept for the encoder and the system, so 24 GiB hold 5 and 7.6 GiB hold 1. More than that gets browsers killed. When `--procs` is more than the CPUs gain from, the tool says so in one line and runs: one software render already uses every core (measured on 8 CPUs: a second process adds about 10 percent, a third nothing).
- When a process stops in the middle (a lost WebGL context, a flat frame), the other processes finish their chunks, and the run ends with `render process N stopped at chunk FIRST-LAST, frame F: why`, where the PNGs are, and `To go on from there:` with the exact command. Nothing drawn is drawn again.
- `--local` is NOT the software renderer of this machine. It starts the installed Chrome in a window, with the machine's graphics card, and its records say `card`. It is refused where no display is. No sandbox machine and no farm machine uses it.
- Values that are no number. After every frame it draws for `film-b` or `loop` the tool asks the page `window.showcase.dev.sanity()`: the count of values in the picture's mean that are no number, read before the lens and the grade (one such sample of 24 stays in the mean, and the lens spreads it into a dark speck that no dark-pixel measure is sure to catch). The count is a count of values, so one pixel can stand for up to 4. Each frame's side file holds `notNumber` and `sanityMs`; the chunk's record holds `notNumber`: `total`, `frames` (`frame` and `count` for each that is not 0), `unread` (frames whose count was never read, such as PNGs left by an older tool), `measure`, `precision` (the page's target: `float`, `half` or `byte`), `blind` (true at `byte`, where the page cannot count and answers 0) and `msAFrame`. A count above 0 prints `NOT A NUMBER: <what> frame F holds N values ...` at once, keeps the frame's PNG under `out/work/master/<what>/not-a-number/`, and sets `mark: "NOT A NUMBER"` in the record. The chunk's intermediate is still written, for looking; the other chunks are drawn; the run then lists every marked chunk and ends with an error. `--collect` lists such a chunk like `FLAT:` (its range, its machine, its frames and counts) and ends with an error while one is there, and says in one more line how many chunks carry no full count. `--encode` refuses a marked chunk. `--ship` carries a marked chunk's record and look under the same names as any chunk's, and says the mark in the commit's first line (`Chunk film-b-002100-002129, NOT A NUMBER in frame 2115 (3): never encoded into a master`) and in the run's `shipped ...` line. The opening is drawn without the engine and is not read. Cost: the page reads the whole mean back in strips of 64 rows and counts on the CPU; see `msAFrame` in a record for the machine at hand. `npm run film` (the rough films) makes the same read after each frame and writes the same `notNumber` and `mark` into its chunk's record with the same loud line, and still encodes: a rough film is for looking.
- `--crf N` and `--part-mb N` stand in for `deliver.master.intermediate.crf` and `partMb` without a change of the config (which would change the build hash); the record says so in `overrides`.
- `--samples S`, `--format F` and `--chunk N` are for tests only. Their chunks are marked `test`, live under `out/master/test/` and `test/` on the branch, and no master is made from them. `--min-psnr N` lowers the floor of the check for such a run only; a master cannot be given it.
- `--what opening` reads `out/work/opening/master/` through `openPack` of `tools/lib/openingpack.mjs` (made by `npm run opening -- --master`) and needs no browser. `--pack DIR` names another folder.
- The page takes a counted tier, `samples-N`, wherever it takes a tier name (`tierSamples` in `src/config.ts`); the master asks for `samples-<deliver.master.samples[what]>`.

### The joining agent

One machine with 4 cores, 7 GB and 15 GB of free disk is enough: film B's intermediates are about 3.2 GB, the loop's about 0.4 GB, each master 0.5 GB, and nothing is decoded to disk. It needs a clone of the same commit, `npm ci` and ffmpeg; no browser. It saves every attachment it is sent into ONE flat folder, in any order, and then:

```
npm run master -- --collect out/work/collected --files /path/to/the/attachments
npm run master -- --encode film-b --from out/work/collected
npm run master -- --encode loop --from out/work/collected
mkdir -p out/work/opening/master
git fetch origin agent/p6-opening-master
for f in manifest.json opening.bin; do git show FETCH_HEAD:out/opening-master/$f > out/work/opening/master/$f; done
npm run master -- --what opening
npm run master -- --encode film-a --from out/work/collected
npm run master -- --split out/deliver/snow-globe-film-4k60.mp4
```

`--collect DIR --files FOLDER` reads every record in FOLDER, moves each chunk it names into `DIR/files/<what>/` when its sha256 is the one the record names, puts a split chunk together from its parts (each part checked against its own sha256, the whole against the record's), and takes the loop's screenshot the same way. A file with another sha256 than its record names is refused and named, a chunk whose parts have not all arrived is listed under `NOT COMPLETE` and the exit code is 1; run the same command again when the rest has come. Files no record names are left where they are and listed. `--keep` copies instead of moving. Without `--no-branches` it also fetches the tip of every remote branch that matches `deliver.master.chunkBranches` (records and looks). It then decodes every chunk it holds, names any flat frame with its chunk and machine, and prints what is there and what is missing, by range.

`--encode film-b|film-a|loop --from DIR` reads the chunks in `out/master/` and in DIR and refuses, with a plain list, when a frame is covered by nothing; when two chunks cover a frame and the hashes of their pixels differ; when the build hash, the samples, the renderer or the calibration picture differ between chunks of one timeline, or a chunk of the film or the loop names no calibration, or the format, the frame rate or the encoder arguments but for the crf differ between any; when a chunk is marked `test`, is off the chunk lines, was rendered for another frame count than `cut/CUTLIST.json` holds, or its mp4 does not match its sha256. Film A is the opening's chunks and then film B's, one encode, one stream.

- The films: two passes of x264 to `deliver.master.final.targetMb` (500) with the sound in it, preset slow, a key frame every 300 frames at most and 60 at least, 3 b frames, 5 reference frames (the most level 5.2 allows at this size), `aq-mode=3:aq-strength=1.4:deblock=-1,-1:no-dct-decimate=1:no-fast-pskip=1`, maxrate and bufsize at the level's own 300 Mbit/s so that they bound nothing. The sound is `audio/final/film-b.wav` or `film-a.wav` as AAC at `deliver.master.audioKbps` (`--sound FILE` names another file), then faststart. A result over the target is encoded once more at the rate that brings it under.
- The loop: no size worry and no sound, so it is encoded to a quality, one pass, with `qcomp=1.0:ipratio=1.0:pbratio=1.0:mbtree=0`: every frame gets the same quantizer, and the last frame and the first are alike. It starts at crf 18 and is encoded again 2 lower while its least frame is under the best second of film B's master (read from film B's record, so encode film B first), down to `minCrf` 16, which is where 480 frames stay under `platform.maxFileMb`.
- Then the proof, on the whole result, printed as one table and kept in the `.json` beside the file: every frame decodes; the frame count; even steps of 1/60 s; the frame rate; yuv420p; the BT.709 tags; High at level 5.2; the reference frames; the key frame spacing; faststart; the size under the target; the sound stream, its rate and its length (none for the loop); no flat frame; and the luma PSNR of EVERY frame against the same frame of the decoded intermediates (least, mean, and which frame is least; the least must reach `deliver.master.final.minPsnr`). For the loop also: its least frame against film B's best second; frame 0 and the last frame, each against its own frame of the decoded intermediates, within `seamDb` (1.5 dB) of each other; and frame 0 against the screenshot PNG itself, the one PNG that still exists on the joining machine.
- All passes: the file is `out/deliver/<deliver.master.names[...]>` with its `.json`. Any FAIL: the file is kept as `.unproven.mp4`, is not a master, and the exit code is 1.
- What may still need tuning after the first real chunks has an option, with the config as its default, printed and written into the record as `overrides`: `--target-mb`, `--preset`, `--keyint`, `--min-keyint`, `--x264`, `--final-min-psnr`, and for the loop `--crf`, `--min-crf`, `--loop-x264`, `--seam-db`. Nothing under `config/` needs to change after the freeze.
- `--encode ... --test [--gaps]` encodes test chunks only, any run of them (with `--gaps`, runs that do not touch), into `out/work/master/test/`.
- `--join` is gone: it says so and names `--encode`.

A master is over 45 MB, so it cannot leave the joining machine as one attachment. `--split FILE` writes `FILE.parts/` with numbered parts of at most 45 MB and `<name>.manifest.json` (the whole file's name, bytes and sha256, and each part's), and prints the messages of at most 10 attachments to send them in. Where they arrive, in one flat folder: `npm run master -- --unsplit DIR [--out FILE]` checks every part against the manifest, puts the file together, checks the whole file's sha256 and refuses to leave a file that does not match.

No black frames: a machine that runs out of memory loses its graphics process, and the page then hands back a black picture without an error. Three guards stand against it. The shared read-back (`Session.grab` and `grabStage` in `tools/lib/page.mjs`, which `seq`, `still`, `film`, `loop` and `master` all use) refuses a picture when the WebGL context was lost at any time since the page opened, or the page or the browser crashed or went away; `Run.frame` and `renderStill` refuse a picture that is one flat value over the whole frame (a diagnostic view may be flat, a frame of the film or the loop never is: `FLAT_FRAMES` in `tools/lib/frames.mjs` would name one by its frame number, and names none). The tool then ends with an error and writes no file and no record. The master decodes every chunk after encoding and keeps its PNGs, unshipped, when a frame is flat or its mean luma is more than 1 code value from its PNG's; `--collect` and `--encode` decode every chunk they take and name the chunk and the machine of a flat frame, and the proof's table has the row `flat frames`, which must be 0. `--procs` is refused for `uhd` when the memory the process may use (its cgroup's limit, not the node's) cannot hold it: about 4.1 GB a render at 24 samples.

The hd films come from the master, not from a second render: `npm run master -- --hd film-b` (or `film-a`) takes every second frame of the encoded, proven `out/deliver` master (`film.fps` over `film.previewFps`), scales it to 1920 x 1080 with the lanczos filter the other tools use, encodes it with the hd film's own settings, copies the master's sound track, and writes `out/work/film-b-hd-final.mp4` with a record that says `scaledFrom` (the master's file, sha256 and size). From there `npm run film -- --which b --from-hd --tier final` and `npm run keep -- --film` do what they did. The card loop is not done this way yet: `npm run loop` joins its own hd chunks and has no flag that takes the 4K loop as its source.

A rule for every tool that hands raw video to ffmpeg: ffmpeg 8 assumes neither a range nor a matrix for untagged raw yuv, and the levels that came out of an encode or a filter fed that way were not the film's (measured 2026-11-26). Raw input must carry `-color_range tv -colorspace bt709 -color_primaries bt709 -color_trc bt709` before `-i`, and a PSNR between two streams must give both the same time base (`settb=1/60,setpts=N`), or frames are paired wrongly.

What it costs, measured on a sandbox VM with 4 cores, 8 GB and no graphics card:

- Drawn by lavapipe (the default since decision 139), film frame 60 at `uhd` (at rest, the globe close) took 128.7 s alone at 24 samples: 5.4 s a sample. In a chunk at 4 samples a frame took 28 s, so about 7 s a frame goes on reading the picture back, the PNG and its hashes. At 12 samples that is about 72 s a frame, 36 minutes a chunk and 72 machine hours for film B; at 16 samples about 93 s a frame. The first frame after a page opens costs 15 to 30 s more. At `hd` the same frame took 15.6 s at 8 samples. `--calibrate` takes 50 to 60 s.
- Lavapipe uses all four cores for one render, so a second process on the same VM gains nothing, and one `uhd` render holds about 3 GB. One render a machine.
- Drawn by SwiftShader, before the change, the same `uhd` frame cost about 70 s a sample and the `hd` frame 18 s a sample; a `uhd` still from the hero camera cost 48 to 83 s a sample at rest and 89 to 136 landed, by season; two renders at once gave 1.24 times the frames an hour of one.
- At 24 samples, `uhd`, lavapipe, main c2a3064f (with the point mend): frames 60 to 69 (a hold) took 159 s for the first and 129.9 s a frame after it; frames 660 to 669 (a change) 226.7 s a frame undisturbed. A chunk of 30 is therefore 65 to 113 minutes, and film B's 3600 frames 130 to 227 machine hours, by how much of it is change (a projection from 20 frames).
- An intermediate at crf 6, preset medium: the key frame 1.72 MB and the others 812 kB on the hold, 1.17 MB and 867 kB on the change, least luma PSNR against the PNGs 48.95 and 50.67 dB. That is 25.3 and 26.3 MB for a chunk of 30 and 3.0 to 3.2 GB for film B (a projection). Crf 4 would be 37 to 39 MB a chunk, crf 8 16 to 17 MB. Encoding and checking 10 frames took 12 s.
- What the intermediate costs the master: a final candidate (crf 18, preset slow) made from the decoded crf 6 intermediate is 0.12 to 0.13 dB further from the PNGs than the same encode made from the PNGs; through crf 8 0.14 to 0.20 dB, through crf 10 0.3 dB.
- The opening, from the real 4K pack (867 frames, 29 chunks): 5.75 MB in all, 0.09 to 0.31 MB a chunk, least luma PSNR 56.9 dB, 208 s of encoding and checking.
- The one encode, measured on 20 frames (10 of the hold, 10 of the change) to the film's rate: two passes at preset slow 13 s, slower 25 s, medium 12 s, so about 40 minutes for film B and 50 for film A at slow and twice that at slower (a projection). Slower was 0.2 to 0.3 dB closer. At 66 Mbit/s the frames were 40.5 dB at least and 43.1 on average from their intermediates.
- In the dark backdrop, at the same size: `-tune grain` kept the least of the PNG's dither (0.47 to 0.54 of it), x264's defaults 0.55 to 0.60, `deblock=-1,-1:no-dct-decimate=1:no-fast-pskip=1` with aq-mode 3 0.58 to 0.63, and the same with `aq-strength=1.4` 0.70 to 0.78, at 0.5 to 1.1 dB less over the whole frame. At true levels none shows steps at native size; stretched 8 times all show 16 px blocks, the last one least.
- The loop at one quantizer for every frame (`qcomp=1.0`, both ratios 1, no mbtree), on the hold: crf 18 gives 47.2 dB for frame 0 and 46.3 to 46.4 for every other, about 0.6 MB a frame; crf 16 48.7 and 47.8, about 0.9 MB a frame; crf 14 would pass `platform.maxFileMb` over 480 frames. With x264's defaults the first frame was 48.1 dB and the last 44.9.
- The final encode is not byte for byte repeatable: four runs of the same command gave four files within 0.2 percent of one size. A master is the file its record names by sha256, not what a second encode would give. The intermediates are repeatable.
